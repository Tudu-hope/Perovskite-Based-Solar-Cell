# Perovskite-Based-Solar-Cell
Designed and simulated a multilayer perovskite solar cell with the architecture **IGZO/SnO₂/WS₂/MA₀.₅FA₀.₅PbI₃/Mg-CuCrO₂/Ni** using **SCAPS-1D**. The study focused on improving photovoltaic performance through device architecture optimization and interface engineering.

The optimized device achieved a **power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.55%**, with a **Voc of 1.1665 V, Jsc of 27.83 mA/cm², and fill factor (FF) of 84.85%**. A systematic analysis was carried out to investigate the effects of **layer thickness, defect density, doping concentration, series resistance, shunt resistance, and operating temperature** on overall device performance.

Particular attention was given to the **WS₂ interfacial layer**, which plays an important role in improving carrier transport and reducing interfacial recombination. The use of the mixed-cation **MA₀.₅FA₀.₅PbI₃ absorber** was also investigated for enhanced charge generation and photovoltaic stability.

The simulation results demonstrate that appropriate **interface engineering, material selection, and parameter optimization** can significantly enhance the performance of perovskite solar cells. The work provides a computational approach for designing high-efficiency multilayer perovskite photovoltaic devices and identifying key parameters that influence their performance.
